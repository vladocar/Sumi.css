Lesson three

Grinding the ink

Notes from a beginners’ calligraphy workshop

Sumi is the carbon ink of East Asian brush painting: soot bound with animal glue and pressed into a stick.

You grind the stick against an inkstone with a few drops of water, and the longer you grind, the deeper the black. Thin, watered washes are called usuzumi. A well-kept stick can be decades old, and many painters say old ink flows more smoothly than new.

What you need

  • An inkstick (sumi)
  • An inkstone (suzuri)
    • a flat end for grinding
    • a deep well for holding ink
  • A brush, a water dropper and a paper weight

One stroke, one breath. You cannot go back over a line.

Workshop rule no. 1

Step by step

  1. Put three drops of water on the flat of the stone.
  2. Grind in small circles for about five minutes.
  3. Push the ink into the well and repeat until it is full.

Theming --s-*

Everything reads from CSS custom properties. Override them on :root, or on any element to theme just that part. Set data-theme="light" or "dark" to force a mode; without it Sumi follows the system setting. Try it live:
